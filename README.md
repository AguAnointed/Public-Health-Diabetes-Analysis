# DATA-DRIVEN INTERVENTION TARGETS: EVALUATING LIFESTYLE DEFICITS IN DIABETES

### Executive Summary
**Question:** Among normoglycemic and metabolically compromised populations, what is the most prevalent lifestyle deficit (diet or physical inactivity), and where should public health interventions be targeted?

**Objective:** To analyse a diabetes indicator dataset with the aim of comparing diet and physical lifestyle factors of non-diabetics to pre-/diabetics.

**Insight:** While diet is a well known driver of diabetes, this analysis shows that physical inactivity is the most prevalent deficit among pre-/diabetics. Thus, public health activities should be geared towards physical movement alongside dietary guidelines.

### Objectives
To use explorative data analysis on a cross-sectional observational survey dataset to determine behavioural differences between normoglycemic and metabolically compromised populations aiding the development, implementation and follow-up of public health policies.


### Methodology
**Data source:** CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS).
**Data pipeline:** Analysed a dataset of 70,692 respondents using Microsoft Excel (COUNTIFS Logic). Transformed the data using Power Query into an interactive Power BI dashboard to visualise categorical lifestyle variables against binary diabetes status.

### Key Findings from the Data
* Prevalence of physical inactivity among pre-/diabetics. 
* Vegetable consumption observed to be the constant healthy habit across all demographics.
* Reduction of fruit consumption among metabolically compromised cohort (prediabetic/diabetic).

### Discussion

Out of every 5 individuals who do not have diabetes, 4 are physically active. 
Among individuals with pre-diabetes or diabetes, 4 out of 9 are NOT physically active.

Out of every 10 persons without diabetes, 7 consume fruits regularly. While half of the people  with pre-/diabetes do not consume fruits.

Among people without diabetes, 5 out of every 6 individuals consume vegetables. Among individuals with pre-/diabetes, exactly 2 out of 3 people consume vegetables.

These show that decline in physical activity is the most prevalent driver of diabetes.

### Conclusion
To translate this evidence into effective policies, the following recommendations must be taken into consideration:

* State governmental-led organisations should prioritise reducing barriers to physical activity. This can be done by implementing policies that ensure mass health movements on specific days of the month. This can be modelled just like the Environmental Sanitation Day in Nigeria, held every last Saturday of each month from dawn till 10:00 AM, ensuring that all residents and business owners clean their premises and clear drainages.
* Update health education to emphasize physical activity equally with diet.
